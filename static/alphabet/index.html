LEARN TO READ HEBREW

Meet the Aleph–Bet

Start at the right and read toward the left. These are the 22 Hebrew letters. Some letters have another form or sound—meet those below.

The alphabet

Final letters

These five letters change shape at the end of a word. Their sound stays the same.

Dots and different sounds

Compare each pair. The dot inside a letter is called a dagesh. In modern Hebrew, it changes the sound of Bet, Kaf, and Pe. The dot above Shin or Sin tells you which sound to use.

More letters can have a dot inside.

A dagesh can also appear in letters such as גּ, דּ, טּ, לּ, מּ, נּ, סּ, צּ, קּ, שּׁ and תּ. In modern Hebrew, it does not give these letters a new consonant sound. The dot in וּ makes the vowel “oo”; וֹ makes “oh”. A dot in final הּ is called a mappiq.

Names and sounds follow common modern Hebrew pronunciation. Vowels beneath the letters are a separate next step.
